About

A closed circle with a public front door.

Ireland produces serious young builders. What it lacks is a room where they can be honest with each other — and a stage where the rest of the country can hear what they learned.

The problem

Young founders here solve the same problems alone.

Every founder in their early twenties re-derives the same answers from scratch: what to charge, which accountant won't overcharge you, whether that term sheet is normal, how to hire a first engineer without a brand. The answers exist. They're just locked inside people who don't have a structured reason to talk to each other.

Meanwhile the public conversation about Irish startups happens at a level of abstraction that helps nobody — panels about ecosystems, not the mechanics of getting a company off the ground.

Structure

Two layers, and the rule that connects them.

Layer one — closed

The inner circle

Membership is by application and personal review. It stays small on purpose: a room only works if everyone in it can be candid, and candour does not scale to a mailing list.

  • Closed peer sessions, off the record
  • A shared resource base — contracts, vendor rates, tooling, playbooks
  • Introductions to customers, hires and investors across the network
  • An obligation to answer other members' questions honestly

Layer two — public

The public programme

Free, open events run under the I Combinator name. Anyone can come — students, first-time builders, people still deciding whether to start something.

  • Showcases of what's actually being built in Ireland
  • Speaker evenings with members and invited guests
  • Meetups in Dublin, Cork, Galway and Limerick
  • No ticket price, no pitch deck required

The rule

Members speak for I Combinator, not for their own companies.

When a member speaks at a public event they represent the community. They are there to hand over something concrete — a number, a mistake, a process — not to recruit, raise or sell.

It is a simple constraint and it does two things. It keeps the public programme worth attending, because nobody is being marketed to. And it protects the inner circle, because members can speak plainly in public without it becoming a company announcement.

What members get

Concretely.

Peer sessions

Small, closed, agenda set by whatever members are actually stuck on that month. No speakers, no slides.

Shared resources

Negotiated vendor rates, legal and hiring templates, tooling credits, and a directory of who has done what before.

Introductions

Warm routes to customers, engineers and investors — made by members who will vouch, which is the only kind that works.

A public platform

Speaking slots at showcases and speaker nights, under the community banner rather than as a solo pitch.

National reach

Members and events across Dublin, Cork, Galway and Limerick — not a single-city club.

Obligation

The one thing you owe back: answer other members properly when they ask, and show up for the public programme occasionally.

Next

Two ways in.

If you're building something and want the closed circle, apply — it's read by a member, and you'll get an answer either way. If you just want to come to things, the public programme is open and free.
